Why the light-mode rail goes grey

Attaché — measured against the 09:11 screenshot, 2026-08-25. Drag the sliders; every panel re-composites the way the app really does.

The one line that does it

ChromeTheme.swift:82 makes the rail “a step deeper” like this:

railBackground = blend(background, toward: .black, by: 0.22)

That multiplies every channel by 0.78 — so the size of the step is proportional to how bright the background already is. On a dark theme the background is around 22/255 and the step removes 5 levels. On a light theme the background is 239/255 and the same 0.22 removes 53 levels. One rule, two completely different results:

Ayu / Ayu Lightpane fillrail fillstepname textsecondarytertiary
Dark(22,24,30)(17,19,23)ΔL* 2.6 9.9:16.8:14.6:1
Light(239,240,241)(186,187,188)ΔL* 18.8 3.3:12.1:11.5:1

Contrast is measured against the rail the text actually sits on. The screenshot agrees to the pixel: its rail samples (186,187,188) and its “main” lines sample 1.5:1. Dark mode does not have this problem — it is a light-mode-only defect.

Second effect, same cause. A darker rail also lowers the ceiling for everything printed on it. Even the primary name is only 3.3:1 there, so no three-level hierarchy below it can be legible. Fixing the rail lifts the ceiling to 4.7:1 — then the secondary and tertiary tones have somewhere to go.

What is not being changed

The double-coat arithmetic (railFillAlpha = extra/(1-a)), the coat colour ramp, the blur, and the rule that “deeper” means toward black on both appearances all stay exactly as they are. The only thing under discussion is how far that one step goes, and whether the two quiet text tones get a floor.
